## 1. Purpose

A beneficiary designation tells Vanguard who should inherit an account when the owner dies. For most retirement accounts and Transfer-on-Death (TOD) brokerage accounts, the named beneficiary inherits **without going through probate**, regardless of what the owner's will may say. Beneficiary designations therefore deserve the same care as a will.

This document covers:
- Which account types accept beneficiary designations
- How to add or update beneficiaries
- Spousal consent rules
- What happens when an account owner dies

## 3. Beneficiary Categories

### 3.1 Primary Beneficiary

Receives the account balance upon the owner's death. Multiple primary beneficiaries are allowed; if so, you must designate the percentage allocated to each (must total 100%).

### 3.2 Contingent Beneficiary

Receives the balance only if all primary beneficiaries have predeceased the account owner or have disclaimed.

### 3.3 Per Stirpes vs Per Capita

- **Per stirpes** ("by the branch") — If a beneficiary predeceases the account owner, that beneficiary's share passes to **their descendants** (e.g., children).
- **Per capita** ("by the head") — If a beneficiary predeceases the account owner, that beneficiary's share is **redistributed equally among the surviving primary beneficiaries**.

Vanguard's default is per stirpes for individual beneficiaries unless the owner explicitly elects per capita.

## 4. Spousal Consent Rules

For Solo 401(k) and certain ERISA-covered retirement plans, federal law requires that the spouse be the sole primary beneficiary unless the spouse provides **notarized written consent** to designate someone else.

For Traditional and Roth IRAs, spousal consent is **not federally required**, but is required by community-property law in: **Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, and Wisconsin**. Account owners residing in these states must obtain a notarized spousal consent form.

## 6. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Naming a minor as primary beneficiary without a custodian.** A minor cannot legally take title; the inherited assets may default to a court-supervised guardianship. Use a UTMA custodian designation or a trust instead.
2. **Failing to update after major life events** — marriage, divorce, birth of a child, death of a beneficiary. Many states automatically void designations to ex-spouses upon divorce, but federal law (ERISA) preempts state law for some plans, so an ex-spouse may inherit despite divorce unless the designation is updated.
3. **Allocations that don't total 100%.** If allocations are missing or incomplete, Vanguard distributes per the most recent valid designation or to the estate.
4. **Naming "my children" without specifying.** Vague designations (e.g., "my issue") create disputes. Always list each beneficiary by name.
5. **Naming the estate intentionally.** This forces probate and may eliminate the stretch IRA option for non-spouse beneficiaries (where it still applies).

## 7. Procedure at Death of Account Owner

### 7.1 Notification

Survivors notify Vanguard via the Estate Settlement team at **800-555-0199** or by mail. A copy of the **certified death certificate** is required.

### 7.2 Verification and Re-titling

Vanguard verifies the death certificate, beneficiary identity (CIP), and proper documentation. Beneficiaries are typically required to provide:
- Certified death certificate
- Beneficiary's identification (driver's license or passport)
- Completed inherited-account paperwork

### 7.3 Inherited IRA Setup

For each non-spouse beneficiary, Vanguard establishes a new Inherited IRA in the beneficiary's name. The account is titled:

> *"[Original Owner's Name], deceased [date], for the benefit of [Beneficiary's Name], beneficiary"*

### 7.4 Distribution Options

Distribution rules depend on:
- Beneficiary type (spouse, eligible designated beneficiary, designated beneficiary, non-designated)
- Original owner's death date relative to required beginning date

For full distribution rules, see **VG-OP-003 (Required Minimum Distributions Guide), Section 5 (Inherited Accounts)**.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does my will override my beneficiary designation?**
No. Beneficiary designations are governing documents that override your will for those specific assets.

**Q: Can I name multiple beneficiaries with different percentages?**
Yes. Allocations must total 100%. Decimals to two places are accepted (e.g., 33.33%, 33.33%, 33.34%).

**Q: What if I don't designate a beneficiary?**
For IRAs, the account passes to the estate per the IRA Custodial Agreement (default). For brokerage accounts without TOD, the account becomes part of the probate estate.

**Q: Can a beneficiary be changed without notifying others?**
Yes — the account owner may change designations at any time without consent of prior beneficiaries (subject to spousal consent rules in Section 4).

**Q: Are beneficiary designations confidential?**
Vanguard does not disclose them to anyone other than the account owner, the owner's legal representative, or — after death — to executors and proven beneficiaries.

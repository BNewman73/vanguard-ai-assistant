## What Is an Expense Ratio?

An expense ratio is the annual cost of operating a mutual fund or ETF, expressed as a percentage of the fund's average net assets. It is deducted directly from fund returns — investors don't write a check or receive a separate bill. Instead, the fee is reflected in the daily NAV calculation.

If a fund's expense ratio is 0.10%, an investor with $10,000 invested pays approximately $10 per year in fund expenses. That cost is spread across every business day of the year and netted against the fund's gross returns before they're passed through to shareholders.

## What's Included in the Expense Ratio

A fund's expense ratio includes:

- **Management fees** — The cost paid to the fund's investment advisor for portfolio management.
- **12b-1 fees** — Marketing and distribution fees (Vanguard does not charge 12b-1 fees on its funds).
- **Other expenses** — Custody, fund accounting, transfer agent services, audit fees, legal fees, and other operating costs.
- **Acquired Fund Fees and Expenses (AFFE)** — For funds-of-funds (such as Target Retirement Funds), the weighted-average expense ratio of the underlying funds.

## What's NOT Included in the Expense Ratio

- **Brokerage commissions paid by the fund** when it trades securities (these reduce returns separately and are reflected in the fund's portfolio turnover-related transaction costs).
- **Bid/ask spreads** on portfolio trading.
- **Investor-level fees** such as the $25 annual account service fee for accounts under $5,000 (waived with e-delivery — see VG-OP-017).
- **Brokerage commissions investors pay** to buy or sell ETF shares on the secondary market (most brokers offer commission-free trades for major Vanguard ETFs).
- **Bid/ask spreads investors pay** when buying or selling ETFs.

## Why Even Small Differences Matter

Expense ratios compound. Consider $100,000 invested for 30 years assuming a 7% gross annual return:

| Expense Ratio | Net Return | Ending Balance | Cost vs 0.03% |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.03% | 6.97% | $759,562 | — |
| 0.10% | 6.90% | $744,419 | $15,143 lower |
| 0.50% | 6.50% | $661,437 | $98,125 lower |
| 1.00% | 6.00% | $574,349 | $185,213 lower |

The same starting investment in a 1.00% ER fund ends with roughly **24% less** than in a 0.03% ER fund — even with identical gross performance. This is one of the strongest arguments for low-cost investing.

## Expense Ratio vs Total Cost of Ownership

For a complete picture of fund cost, also consider:

1. **Trading costs** for ETFs: bid/ask spreads (typically 0.01%–0.05% for liquid Vanguard ETFs)
2. **Tax cost** for taxable accounts: turnover-driven distributions
3. **Account fees**: the $25 annual fee on small mutual fund accounts (waived with e-delivery)
4. **Advisory fees** if you use Personal Advisor Services (see VG-OP-008): currently 0.30%–0.40% on top of the underlying fund expense ratios

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Where can I find a fund's current expense ratio?**
The expense ratio is in every prospectus, on the fund's page at investor.vanguard.com, on Form 1099-DIV documentation (see VG-OP-010), and on quarterly account statements (see VG-OP-017).

**Q: Do expense ratios change?**
Yes. Vanguard reviews them annually. Because Vanguard operates at-cost, expense ratios generally trend downward as fund assets grow and economies of scale improve.

**Q: Are expense ratios negotiable?**
No. Every shareholder pays the same expense ratio regardless of account size, except where qualifying for a different share class with a lower published expense ratio.

**Q: How are expense ratios deducted?**
Daily, in the NAV calculation. There is never a separate transaction or charge.

**Q: Does the expense ratio include sales loads?**
No. Vanguard funds do not charge sales loads. If a fund prospectus shows sales charges (loads), those are separate from the expense ratio.

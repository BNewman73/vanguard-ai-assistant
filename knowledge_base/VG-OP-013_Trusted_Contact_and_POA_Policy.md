## 1. Purpose

This policy describes two safeguards available to Vanguard account holders that protect against financial exploitation and ensure continuity of account access during incapacity:

1. **Trusted Contact Person (TCP)** — A non-binding contact Vanguard may reach out to in narrow circumstances
2. **Power of Attorney (POA)** — A legal document granting another person authority to transact on your behalf

These are distinct instruments. TCP gives Vanguard someone to *call*; POA gives someone authority to *act*.

## 2. Trusted Contact Person

### 2.1 What Is a Trusted Contact?

A Trusted Contact Person is an individual you authorize Vanguard to contact in limited situations. The TCP has **no authority** to access account information, transact, or make decisions on your behalf — they are simply a contact point.

FINRA rules require broker-dealers to make reasonable efforts to obtain a TCP for all retail brokerage accounts of investors age 18+. Vanguard prompts new and existing clients to designate one but does not require it.

### 2.2 When Vanguard May Contact a TCP

Vanguard may reach out to your TCP only to:
- Confirm specifics of your current contact information
- Inquire about your health status if Vanguard has a reasonable belief of cognitive decline
- Identify any legal guardian, executor, trustee, or agent under power of attorney
- Discuss possible financial exploitation if Vanguard reasonably suspects exploitation, abuse, or undue influence

The TCP may be contacted only by Vanguard agents trained in senior-investor protection.

### 2.3 Who Should You Designate?

A TCP should be:
- Age 18 or older
- Someone you trust who knows your general financial picture
- Someone reachable (active phone, current address)
- **Not** someone who would gain financially from your incapacity (avoid sole heirs as TCPs)

Common TCP choices: adult child, sibling, longtime friend, attorney, accountant.

### 2.4 How to Designate a TCP

Online: **My Accounts → Profile → Trusted Contact**. Provide:
- Full legal name
- Phone number
- Email (preferred)
- Mailing address
- Relationship to you

Designation is effective immediately. You may add up to **two** TCPs and remove or replace them at any time.

### 2.5 FINRA Rule 2165 — Temporary Hold

FINRA Rule 2165 authorizes a broker-dealer to place a **temporary hold** on disbursements from an account when there is reasonable belief that the account holder (age 65+, or 18+ with mental/physical impairment) is being financially exploited.

Hold details:
- Initial hold: up to **15 business days** from the date the firm first reasonably believes exploitation is occurring
- Extension: up to **25 additional business days** if the firm's review supports continued belief of exploitation
- Maximum total: **40 business days** absent court order or formal investigation
- During the hold, the firm must notify the TCP (and the customer) within 2 business days

Holds may be lifted earlier if:
- Vanguard's investigation concludes no exploitation is occurring
- A court order or law-enforcement directive overrides the hold
- The customer revokes any suspect authorization

For broader fraud-prevention controls including transaction monitoring and red flags, see **VG-OP-011 (Security and Fraud Prevention Policy)**.

## 3. Power of Attorney (POA)

### 3.1 What Is a POA?

A Power of Attorney is a legal document in which one person (the **principal**) grants another person (the **agent** or **attorney-in-fact**) legal authority to act on the principal's behalf. The agent's authority extends only to the powers granted in the document.

Common POA types Vanguard accepts:

| Type | Effective | Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Durable POA** | Immediately or upon signing; remains valid if principal becomes incapacitated | Most common; provides continuity |
| **Springing POA** | Only upon a triggering event (typically incapacity, certified by physicians) | When principal wants to retain control until necessary |
| **Limited POA** | Covers specified transactions only (e.g., a single sale) | Targeted; expires on completion |
| **General POA** | Broad authority but **non-durable** (terminates on incapacity) | Less useful for retirement-account continuity |

Vanguard does **not** accept "Springing POA" without satisfactory medical certification documenting incapacity.

### 3.2 Submitting a POA to Vanguard

Required documentation:
1. **Original or certified copy** of the POA document (notarized, signed by principal)
2. **POA Acceptance Form** (Vanguard form) signed by the agent, acknowledging fiduciary duties
3. **Government-issued photo ID** for the agent
4. For durable POAs that name a successor agent: documentation of how successor authority is triggered

Submit by:
- **Mail:** PO Box 982901, Valley Forge, PA 19482 — Attn: POA Operations
- **Upload:** investor.vanguard.com → Profile → Authorizations
- **Walk-in:** any Vanguard investor center (limited locations)

Vanguard's review and approval typically takes **5–10 business days**. The agent receives written confirmation of approved authority.

### 3.3 Scope of Agent Authority

A POA agent at Vanguard may, depending on the POA's terms:
- View account balances, holdings, and statements
- Place trades (subject to suitability for older or incapacitated principals)
- Initiate transfers between principal-owned accounts
- Withdraw funds (typically to a bank account in the principal's name; third-party transfers usually denied)
- Update beneficiary designations (requires written principal consent unless explicitly granted in POA)

Limitations Vanguard imposes by default unless POA explicitly authorizes:
- Cannot change beneficiaries
- Cannot transfer funds to the agent's own account
- Cannot open new accounts in principal's name
- Cannot change Trusted Contact Person

### 3.4 Termination of POA

A POA terminates on:
- Death of the principal (immediately, regardless of POA terms)
- Revocation by the principal (in writing)
- Court order
- For non-durable POAs: incapacity of principal
- Expiration of stated term

Notify Vanguard of any termination in writing. Until Vanguard receives notice, transactions executed in good faith by the agent under the POA remain valid.

## 4. Comparison: TCP vs POA

| Feature | Trusted Contact | Power of Attorney |
|---|---|---|
| Legal authority to act? | No | Yes |
| Vanguard may contact? | Yes (limited circumstances) | Yes (any time) |
| May see account information? | No | Yes |
| May place trades? | No | Yes (per POA terms) |
| Document required? | None (just designation) | Notarized POA + Vanguard form |
| Typical reason to designate? | Senior protection, contact during incapacity | Account continuity during incapacity |
| Removable by account owner? | Anytime | Anytime, in writing |

Most account holders **should have both**. The TCP is a low-friction safeguard; the POA is the legal instrument for actual continuity.

## 5. Joint Accounts and Trusts

For joint brokerage accounts: each owner may transact independently, so POA is generally unnecessary unless both owners are incapacitated. Each owner should designate their own TCP.

For trust accounts: trustees act under the trust instrument, not via POA. Successor trustees take over per trust terms. A trust account typically does not need a POA.

## 6. Fraud and Exploitation Concerns

Vanguard treats POAs as both a tool and a fraud risk vector. We may decline to act on a POA if:
- The document appears altered or forged
- The agent's behavior suggests coercion of the principal
- Transactions are inconsistent with the principal's known goals or risk tolerance
- The POA was created shortly before significant transactions in unusual ways
- The agent is unable to answer basic questions about the principal's circumstances

If Vanguard declines, we may notify the principal directly, the TCP, and/or initiate the FINRA Rule 2165 temporary hold (Section 2.5). Adult Protective Services may be contacted in egregious cases. See VG-OP-011 (Security and Fraud Prevention Policy).

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a POA if I have a will?**
Yes. A will governs after death; a POA governs during life. Both are recommended.

**Q: Will Vanguard automatically contact my TCP if I miss a withdrawal?**
No. The TCP is contacted only in narrow exploitation- or capacity-related circumstances, not for routine matters.

**Q: My parent has Vanguard accounts and dementia. Can I just call and discuss?**
Not without legal authority. Without a POA or guardianship, Vanguard cannot share information beyond confirming the account exists. Encourage your parent to designate you as TCP and POA agent before further decline.

**Q: Can the agent under POA close my account?**
Only if the POA grants account-closure authority. Many POAs are silent on this; Vanguard typically declines closure without explicit grant.

**Q: How is a POA different from a successor on a TOD designation?**
TOD (Transfer on Death) takes effect at death. POA takes effect during life. Both are typically needed for full continuity. See VG-OP-002 for TOD detail.

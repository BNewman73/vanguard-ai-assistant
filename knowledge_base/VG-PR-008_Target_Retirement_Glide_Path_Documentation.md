## 1. Purpose

This document explains the asset-allocation glide path used by all Vanguard Target Retirement Funds (the "TRFs"). It is referenced by the prospectus of every TRF (e.g., VG-PR-004 for the Target Retirement 2050 Fund) and is the authoritative source for current and projected allocations. Investors, plan sponsors, and advisors should consult this document for any glide-path-specific question.

## 2. Glide Path Overview

A glide path is a pre-defined trajectory that adjusts the equity-to-bond ratio of a target-date fund as it approaches and passes its target retirement year. The Vanguard glide path is designed around these principles:

- **Single trajectory.** All TRFs share the same glide path. The only difference between funds is the year on the path.
- **Continuous descent.** Allocations adjust gradually each year, not in step changes.
- **No "to retirement" / "through retirement" split.** Vanguard uses a single "through retirement" approach: equity exposure continues to decline for approximately 7 years past the target year before reaching the landing allocation.
- **Landing allocation: 30% equity / 70% bonds.** Reached approximately 7 years after the target year. From that point, the allocation is held constant — equivalent to the Target Retirement Income Fund.

## 4. Underlying Building Blocks

Each TRF holds four underlying Vanguard index funds in the proportions shown above:

1. **U.S. Stocks** — Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund (VTMSI/VTMSA/VTMS). See VG-PR-001.
2. **International Stocks** — Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund (VTISI/VTISA/VTIS).
3. **U.S. Bonds** — Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund (VTBMI/VTBMA/VTBM). See VG-PR-003.
4. **International Bonds** — Vanguard Total International Bond Index Fund (USD-hedged).

The international bond allocation is **fully USD-hedged** to remove currency volatility from the bond sleeve. The international stock allocation is **unhedged** because long-term equity returns are well-diversified by currency exposure, and hedging costs would be a meaningful drag.

## 5. Rebalancing Rules

### 5.1 Quarterly Rebalancing

Each TRF is rebalanced quarterly back to its current target weights. The rebalancing dates are the last business day of March, June, September, and December.

### 5.2 Tolerance Bands

If any single underlying fund weight drifts more than **2 percentage points** from its target between scheduled rebalances, the portfolio managers may rebalance early at their discretion. This rule is designed to limit drift during fast-moving markets.

### 5.3 Annual Glide Step

On approximately **March 1 of each calendar year**, the target weights themselves are adjusted by one step along the glide path. The step size depends on position on the trajectory but is typically 1–1.5 percentage points of equity reduction per year for funds within 25 years of their target date.

## 6. Landing and Income Fund Merger

Approximately 7 years after each TRF's target year, the Fund reaches the 30/70 landing allocation. At that point:

- **Notice.** The Board of Trustees evaluates whether to merge the Fund into the Vanguard Target Retirement Income Fund (VTRIN). If merger is approved, shareholders receive at least **60 days' written notice** before the effective date.
- **Tax treatment.** The merger is structured as a tax-free reorganization under Internal Revenue Code §368(a)(1)(F). Cost basis carries over; no taxable event for shareholders.
- **Continuation.** Investors who do not wish to remain in the Income Fund may exchange to a different Vanguard fund at no cost (subject to the 30-day frequent-trading policy).

## 7. Common Investor Scenarios

### 7.1 "I'm 25 years from retirement" (e.g., age 40 in 2026, retiring around 2051)

Use the **Vanguard Target Retirement 2050 Fund (VG-PR-004)**. The 2055 Fund is also a reasonable choice if you plan to work longer or want a more aggressive trajectory.

### 7.2 "I'm 5 years from retirement" (e.g., retiring around 2031)

Use the **Vanguard Target Retirement 2030 Fund**. Current allocation: approximately 55% equity / 45% bonds.

### 7.3 "I'm in retirement and want stability"

Use the **Vanguard Target Retirement Income Fund (VTRIN)**, which holds the 30/70 landing allocation in perpetuity.

### 7.4 "I want to mix and match"

You may hold multiple TRFs simultaneously to fine-tune your average target date. For example, holding 50% of your assets in the 2045 Fund and 50% in the 2055 Fund yields an effective target year of 2050 with a slightly different trajectory than the 2050 Fund itself.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does Vanguard tactically deviate from the glide path during market stress?**
No. The glide path is strategic, not tactical. There is no market-timing component.

**Q: Why does Vanguard hedge international bonds but not international stocks?**
Currency volatility dominates the volatility of foreign bonds, so hedging meaningfully reduces fixed-income volatility without giving up much expected return. For stocks, currency volatility is small relative to equity volatility, and hedging would impose persistent costs.

**Q: Can I customize the glide path?**
Not within the TRFs themselves. For customization, consider Vanguard Personal Advisor Services (see VG-OP-008).

**Q: How often does the underlying fund mix change?**
The four-fund mix has been stable since 2015. Material changes require Board approval and shareholder notice.
